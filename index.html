HER STORY

A Life Dedicated to Science

Marie Curie was born Maria Skłodowska in Warsaw, Poland, in 1867. From an early age, she showed a strong interest in learning, particularly in mathematics and science. At a time when opportunities for women in higher education were limited, she remained determined to continue her studies.

In 1891, she moved to Paris and studied at the Sorbonne, where she developed her scientific career. She later met Pierre Curie, a physicist who became both her research partner and husband. Together, they investigated the mysterious phenomenon of radioactivity.

Marie and Pierre Curie's research led to the discovery of two new elements, polonium and radium. Their work helped establish radioactivity as an important area of scientific research. Marie developed methods for studying radioactive materials and devoted years to careful experimental work.

Marie Curie's achievements were remarkable for both their scientific importance and their historical significance. She became the first woman to receive a Nobel Prize and remains the only person to receive Nobel Prizes in two different scientific fields. Her determination and dedication continue to inspire scientists around the world.

KEY MOMENTS

Marie Curie's Timeline

1867

Born in Warsaw

Marie Skłodowska was born on November 7, 1867, in Warsaw, Poland.

1891

Moves to Paris

Marie moved to Paris to continue her education at the University of Paris, commonly known as the Sorbonne.

1898

Discovery of Polonium and Radium

Marie and Pierre Curie announced the discovery of the elements polonium and radium.

1903

First Nobel Prize

Marie Curie, Pierre Curie, and Henri Becquerel received the Nobel Prize in Physics for their work on radiation.

1911

Second Nobel Prize

Marie received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for her discoveries involving radium and polonium and her study of their properties.

1934

Her Legacy Lives On

Marie Curie died on July 4, 1934, but her scientific discoveries continued to influence physics, chemistry, medicine, and research.
